How the projection works

The calculator runs in two phases. While you work, your savings grow monthly at the pre-retirement return and you add your monthly saving. After you retire, each year you withdraw the income you asked for, minus any pension or government benefit, grown for inflation, while the rest earns the post-retirement return.

The needed at retirement figure is the lump sum that would fund that inflation-adjusted income until your planning age, given the post-retirement return. If your projected savings fall short, the calculator shows the extra monthly saving that closes the gap by your retirement date.

Levers that move the result most

  • Retirement age. Working two more years adds two years of saving and growth and removes two years of withdrawals.
  • Saving rate. Capturing a full employer match is the highest guaranteed return most people will ever get.
  • Spending in retirement. Many retirees spend 70–80% of their pre-retirement income; your number may differ.
  • Fees. A 1% annual fee can leave a portfolio about a quarter smaller after 30 years. Low-cost index funds help; see our ETF guide.

Retirement FAQ

What return should I assume?

Many planners use 5–7% before retirement for a stock-heavy portfolio and 4–5% after, as portfolios usually become more conservative. Test a pessimistic case too.

Does this include taxes?

No. Enter the income you want before tax, or raise the target to cover tax on traditional 401(k), IRA or RRSP withdrawals.
