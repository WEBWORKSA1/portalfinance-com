How this mortgage calculator works

Your monthly payment has up to five parts. Principal and interest are fixed for a fixed-rate loan and use the standard amortization formula: M = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12) and n the number of monthly payments.

On top of that sit property tax (entered as a yearly percentage of the home's price), homeowners insurance, private mortgage insurance (PMI) when you put down less than 20%, and any HOA dues. Many lenders collect tax and insurance through an escrow account, so this full figure is closer to what leaves your bank account each month.

Why extra payments matter so much

Early in a 30-year loan, most of each payment is interest. On a $360,000 loan at 6.5%, the first payment sends about $1,950 to interest and only about $325 to principal. Every extra dollar you pay goes straight to principal, which shrinks the balance that future interest is charged on. Try adding $200 a month above: the saving is usually tens of thousands of dollars and several years.

Rules of thumb lenders use

  • 28% front-end ratio: housing costs no more than about 28% of gross monthly income.
  • 36–43% back-end ratio: all debt payments, including the mortgage, under roughly 36% (conventional) to 43% (many qualified mortgages).
  • 20% down avoids PMI on conventional loans; PMI usually costs 0.3%–1.5% of the loan per year.

Mortgage FAQ

What interest rate should I enter?

Use the rate from a written Loan Estimate if you have one. Otherwise use a current average for your loan type and credit tier, then test a rate one point higher to see how sensitive the payment is.

When does PMI go away?

On most conventional US loans you can ask to cancel PMI once the balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and it ends automatically at 78%. FHA mortgage insurance follows different rules.

Is a 15-year mortgage better than a 30-year?

A 15-year loan usually has a lower rate and far less total interest, but a much higher payment. A 30-year loan with voluntary extra payments keeps flexibility if your income drops. Run both here and compare total interest.
