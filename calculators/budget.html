What counts as a need, a want and a saving

Needs are the bills you'd still pay if you lost your job tomorrow: housing, utilities, basic groceries, transport to work, insurance, child care and the minimum payments on debts. Wants are everything that improves life but could pause: restaurants, streaming, upgrades, travel. Savings include your emergency fund, retirement contributions beyond any payroll deduction, and any payment above a debt's minimum.

The 50/30/20 split was popularised as a simple rule of thumb, not a law. In high-cost cities, needs often run 60% or more; in that case shrink wants first and protect at least 10–15% for savings. If you carry high-interest debt, push the third bucket well past 20% until it's gone.

Make it automatic

  1. Have your savings transfer leave on payday, before you can spend it.
  2. Put fixed bills on autopay from one account.
  3. Spend wants from a separate account or card, so the balance tells you what's left.

The full system, including zero-based budgeting, is in How to build a budget that survives real life.

Budget FAQ

Is 50/30/20 based on gross or net income?

Net. Use take-home pay after taxes and payroll deductions. If your employer deducts retirement contributions, you can count them toward the 20%.

Where does my emergency fund go?

In the 20% savings bucket. Build it to at least one month of needs first, then aim for three to six months. See our emergency fund guide.
