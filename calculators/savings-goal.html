Where to keep money for a goal

Match the account to the deadline. Money you'll need within about three years shouldn't ride the stock market, because a 20–30% drop right before you need it can wreck the plan. For short goals, use a high-yield savings account, money market fund, CDs or Treasury bills. For goals five or more years away, a balanced mix of stocks and bonds is reasonable.

The required monthly figure assumes interest compounds monthly at the rate you enter, and that you deposit at the end of each month. Raising the rate from 1% to 4% on a four-year goal typically trims the monthly requirement by a few percent; extending the deadline does far more.

Common goals and typical targets

  • Emergency fund: three to six months of essential expenses.
  • Home down payment: 3–20% of the price plus 2–5% for closing costs.
  • Car: enough to pay cash, or at least 20% down to avoid owing more than it's worth.

Savings goal FAQ

Should I save or pay off debt first?

Build a starter emergency fund of about one month of expenses, then attack debt above roughly 7–8% interest, then return to bigger goals. Use the debt payoff calculator to compare.

Are savings account rates guaranteed?

Variable savings rates can change at any time. CDs and Treasury bills lock a rate for their term.
