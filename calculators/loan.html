How loan payments are calculated

Installment loans use the same amortization formula as mortgages: a fixed payment where the interest share shrinks as the balance falls. The interest each month is simply the balance times the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12). Whatever is left of your payment reduces principal.

The term is the biggest lever after the rate. Stretching a $30,000 car loan at 8.5% from 60 to 84 months drops the payment by roughly $140 but adds nearly $3,000 in interest, and leaves you owing more than the car is worth for longer.

Three ways to pay less

  • Shorten the term to the shortest payment you can comfortably afford.
  • Raise your credit score before applying. Moving up one credit tier can cut several points off a personal-loan APR. See how credit scores work.
  • Pay extra toward principal. Confirm your lender has no prepayment penalty and applies extra amounts to principal.

Loan FAQ

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

The interest rate is the cost of borrowing the principal. APR adds most required fees, such as origination fees, and expresses the total as a yearly rate, so APR is the better number for comparing offers.

Does this work for student loans?

Yes for standard fixed repayment. Income-driven plans calculate payments from your income instead, so use your servicer's tools for those.
