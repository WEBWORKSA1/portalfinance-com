The formula behind compounding

For a single deposit, the future value is FV = P(1 + r/n)nt: principal P, annual rate r, compounding periods per year n, and years t. Regular contributions add a growing stack of smaller deposits, each compounding for a shorter time. This calculator works month by month so it can handle contributions that rise each year.

The key insight is that time does more of the work than the rate. At 7%, money roughly doubles every 10 years (the rule of 72: 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10.3). Someone who invests $500 a month from age 25 to 35 and then stops can end up with more at 65 than someone who invests $500 a month from 35 to 65, because the early money has three extra doublings.

Picking a realistic return

  • High-yield savings or CDs: use the quoted APY; it is roughly guaranteed up to deposit-insurance limits.
  • A diversified stock index fund: long-run US returns have averaged around 10% a year before inflation, about 6–7% after. Planners often use 5–7% to stay conservative.
  • Bonds: roughly the current yield of the fund, which changes with interest rates.

Returns are never smooth. A real portfolio will have down years. The line here shows the average path, not a promise. Learn more in Compound interest explained.

Compound interest FAQ

Does daily compounding make a big difference?

Less than people expect. At 5%, daily compounding yields about 5.13% a year versus 5.12% for monthly. The rate and the time invested matter far more.

Should I subtract inflation?

To see results in today's dollars, enter a real return (your expected return minus expected inflation, for example 7% minus 3% = 4%).
