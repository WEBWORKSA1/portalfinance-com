      Snowball or avalanche?

      Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and throw every spare dollar at one target. When that target is paid off, its payment rolls into the next target, so your payoff power grows like a snowball either way. The only difference is the order.

      • Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first. It is mathematically optimal: it always costs the least interest and is never slower.
      • Snowball targets the smallest balance first. You clear whole accounts sooner, and research on consumer debt repayment suggests those early wins help people stick with the plan.

      If the two results above are close, pick the snowball for momentum. If the avalanche saves real money, usually when a large balance carries the highest rate, take the avalanche. The full reasoning is in our snowball vs. avalanche guide.

      Debt payoff FAQ

      Should I include my mortgage?

      Usually not. Mortgages carry low rates and long terms; most plans focus on consumer debt first. Add it if you want a complete debt-free date.

      What if a minimum payment doesn't cover the interest?

      Then that balance grows each month and the calculator may show "never" for minimums only. Prioritise that debt or call the lender about a hardship plan.
