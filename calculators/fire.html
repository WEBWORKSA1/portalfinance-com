The math of financial independence

Your FIRE number is yearly spending divided by your withdrawal rate. At a 4% rate, that's 25 times spending: $50,000 a year needs $1.25 million. The 4% figure comes from research on historical US market returns (often called the Trinity study), which found a 4% initial withdrawal, raised with inflation, lasted 30 years in most historical periods.

Early retirees need money to last 40–60 years, so many use 3.25–3.5% instead. Each half-point lower raises the target meaningfully, so test both. Because this calculator uses a real return, all figures are in today's dollars.

Savings rate beats everything

The share of income you invest determines how fast you get there more than investment returns do. Saving 10% of take-home pay implies roughly 40+ years of work; saving 50% shortens that to about 17 years at a 5% real return, because you're both building the portfolio faster and needing a smaller one.

FIRE FAQ

What's the difference between Lean, Fat and Coast FIRE?

Lean FIRE targets a minimal budget, Fat FIRE a generous one. Coast FIRE means you've saved enough that growth alone reaches your target by a normal retirement age, so you only need to earn your current expenses.

Is a 4% withdrawal rate safe?

It held up for 30-year retirements across most of US history, but nothing is guaranteed. Flexible spending in bad years improves the odds considerably.
