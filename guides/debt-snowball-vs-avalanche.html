Debt snowball vs. avalanche: which payoff method wins?

One method saves the most money. The other is more likely to keep you going. Here's how to choose, with numbers.

By the PortalFinance Editorial TeamUpdated November 12, 20267-minute read

What both methods share

Both strategies follow the same three rules: pay the minimum on every debt, put every spare dollar toward one target debt, and when that debt is gone, roll its whole payment into the next target. That roll-over is the "snowball" effect, and both methods get it. The only difference is the order of targets.

The avalanche: highest interest rate first

Target the debt with the highest APR, regardless of balance. Because you're always paying down the most expensive money first, the avalanche produces the lowest total interest and never a slower debt-free date. It's the mathematically optimal answer.

The snowball: smallest balance first

Target the smallest balance, regardless of rate. You close whole accounts sooner, which means fewer bills, fewer due dates and visible progress. Research on how consumers repay debt, including studies published in peer-reviewed marketing and psychology journals, suggests that closing accounts early helps people stay motivated, and a plan you finish beats a perfect plan you abandon.

Side-by-side example

Three debts, minimum payments of $575 in total, plus $250 a month extra:

DebtBalanceAPRMinimum
Store card$1,20029.99%$40
Credit card$6,50024.99%$195
Car loan$14,0007.9%$340

In this case the store card has both the smallest balance and the highest rate, so both methods attack it first and the paths only diverge slightly. That's common: small store and retail cards often carry the highest rates. The difference grows when a large balance carries the top rate, for example a $15,000 card at 27% next to a $900 medical bill at 0%. Then the avalanche can save hundreds or thousands.

Enter your own debts in the debt payoff calculator to see both plans, your debt-free date and the interest each one costs.

Rule of thumb: If the avalanche saves less than about 3% of your total debt, choose the snowball for momentum. If it saves more, choose the avalanche and celebrate milestones some other way.

Ways to speed up either plan

  • Lower the rate. A 0% balance-transfer card (watch the 3–5% transfer fee and the end date) or a lower-rate consolidation loan cuts interest, as long as you stop adding new debt.
  • Call your issuer. Asking for a lower APR works more often than people expect, especially with a good payment history.
  • Find the extra. Every $100 a month added to the target debt shortens the plan noticeably. Use the budget calculator to find it.
  • Protect the plan. Keep a starter emergency fund so a flat tyre doesn't go back on the card.

When to get outside help

If minimum payments take more than about 20% of your take-home pay, or you're using one card to pay another, talk to a non-profit credit counselling agency about a debt management plan before considering debt settlement or bankruptcy. Be wary of companies that charge upfront fees or tell you to stop paying creditors.
