Safety

Investment scam red flags

Scammers are polished, patient and increasingly use fake apps, AI-generated video and cloned websites. The red flags, though, haven't changed much.

By the PortalFinance Editorial TeamUpdated November 12, 20267-minute read

The scale of the problem

US Federal Trade Commission data show consumers reported losing more than $12.5 billion to fraud in 2024, and investment scams were the costliest category at $5.7 billion. Many losses go unreported, and the people targeted include experienced investors.

Ten red flags

  1. Guaranteed high returns with little or no risk. Real investments don't promise both.
  2. Pressure to act now: "only three spots left", "the price goes up tomorrow".
  3. Unregistered sellers or products. Legitimate advisers and most offerings are registered and searchable.
  4. Payment by crypto, wire, gift card or payment app to an individual or an unfamiliar account.
  5. An online friend or match who steers you toward investing, often on a platform they recommend. This is the pattern behind "pig-butchering" scams.
  6. A slick app showing big gains you can't withdraw, or "taxes" and "fees" demanded before a withdrawal.
  7. Celebrity or expert endorsements in ads, often deepfaked video or fake news articles.
  8. Secrecy: "don't tell your bank", "this is exclusive".
  9. Complex jargon with no clear explanation of how the money is actually made.
  10. Unsolicited contact by phone, text, DM or email, including messages that impersonate a bank, regulator or well-known firm.

Rule of thumb: If someone you've never met in person is helping you invest, stop. If you can't verify it independently, don't send money.

How to verify an offer

  • Look up the person and firm on FINRA BrokerCheck and the SEC adviser database, or your country's regulator (for example the FCA register in the UK or the Canadian Securities Administrators' national registration search).
  • Contact the company using a phone number or website you find yourself, never one supplied in the message.
  • Search the company name plus "scam", "complaint" or "fraud".
  • Ask a trusted friend or professional. Scammers rely on isolation.

Watch for impersonation

Fraudsters copy the names, logos and websites of real financial firms, and sometimes regulators. PortalFinance.com will never ask you for passwords, one-time codes, crypto transfers or payment to "unlock" a plan or prize. Our free plan and contests never require payment.

If you've been targeted

  1. Stop sending money and stop contact with the scammer.
  2. Call your bank or card issuer immediately to try to stop or reverse payments.
  3. Report it in the US at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the FBI's IC3; in Canada to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre; elsewhere to your national fraud agency.
  4. Change passwords and enable two-factor authentication on financial accounts.
  5. Beware "recovery" services that promise to get your money back for a fee. That's often the same scammers returning.
