How to read today's market without overreacting

Daily moves are mostly noise. On any single day it is close to a coin flip whether the S&P 500 rises or falls, yet over decades it has delivered strong gains. If you invest for goals years away, daily swings shouldn't change your plan. Use this page to stay informed, and use the compound interest calculator to keep the long view.

  • Indices such as the S&P 500 or Nasdaq 100 summarize large groups of stocks.
  • Heatmaps show breadth: whether gains are broad or concentrated in a few giant companies.
  • The economic calendar flags events like interest-rate decisions and inflation reports that often cause volatility.

Market data is provided by third parties for information only and may be delayed. Nothing on this page is a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Charts and widgets by TradingView; crypto data by CoinGecko; exchange rates via Frankfurter from the European Central Bank.
